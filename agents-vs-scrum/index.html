Scrum team or direct agents: no clear difference in price for the code.

A small case study. One owner, one product: a few days of agents working through a scrum-style queue with a planner and a reviewer, then a week of agents briefed directly by one lead session. The price of each way, from the records, side by side.

By Guy Eden

What we wanted to find out

When AI agents write your software, should you copy a human scrum team: a planner, an architect, workers and an independent reviewer passing work along a queue? Or should one lead session brief plain agents and merge their work?

Our bet was that structure buys quality: a reviewer who never saw the code should catch what its author misses, at some cost. The queue was switched off after a few days as too slow and expensive; the direct agents ran on alone from then. This page puts the two periods' records side by side.

The direct lead session's own cost is not included; the Scrum planner and reviewer are. Different tasks, models and periods. Scrum's first day is excluded from the figures; including it raises its price per commit to up to $2.16.

Quality: no clear quality advantage detected

A small set of the same items was done both ways and graded blind by one reviewer model, which could not tell which side a change came from. It found no clear quality advantage for either way; a small blind check cannot tell a real difference from noise, and that is not a finding that the two are alike.

Every role and the model it ran on

Direct

Work goes in the queue, an agent takes an item, the main session integrates: it briefs, reads each diff, rebases, runs the ratchet, merges (fast-forward) and runs the merge check.

Scrum

Personas claim items; scripts do the waiting. Retired after a few days.

Both shapes consume the same queue of items; it is drawn once in each so the diagrams stay apart. Shape says the kind of role: circle the human, hexagon a script, shield the reviewer, blob an agent, rounded diamond the main session. Colour says the model (blue Sonnet, purple Fable, grey no model, not recorded, or mixed). A dashed outline marked sometimes is a step that runs only for some items. The direct agents' model varied by day (Opus, Sonnet, Haiku); the nightly review, on Fable, is not drawn. Models are the ones recorded in the agent definitions and the token ledger; where none is recorded, the block says so.

What this does and does not show

One owner, one product, a few days of each way, with no controlled comparison between the two periods. On this record neither way was clearly cheaper or faster for the code it produced; the queue was switched off for being slow to merge an item, not for being dearer. It says nothing about agent workflows in general, other teams, other models or larger, entangled work.

Not a controlled experiment. Data: the harness's token ledger (one line per agent), the queue's item files and spawn ledger, and git history, late October.
