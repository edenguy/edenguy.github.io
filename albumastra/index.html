Reel 1 · The Problem

Turning an album into a film is still a manual cutting-room job

PAIN 01

A real family album easily runs 200–400 photos and clips — sorting that by capture date and quality by hand is a multi-hour task before you've cut a single scene.

PAIN 02

Raw phone video routinely runs 30–90+ seconds per clip. Drop it in unedited and a five-minute film becomes an unwatchable half hour.

PAIN 03

Licensing a soundtrack you can actually publish — without a future takedown — is its own research project most people skip, then wonder why the clip's raw noise is all you hear.

PAIN 04

A cut for the living-room TV and one for a phone screen are two different aspect ratios, two exports, two rounds of manual timing.

Reel 2 · The Cut

Six stages, one pass, fully reproducible

Each frame below is a real, separate stage in the pipeline — cacheable and independently re-runnable. Nothing in this reel calls an AI model; a dedicated test suite audits the runtime for exactly that.

FRAME 01

Ingest

download_album.py

Enumerates every item in the album's own Google Photos order and downloads concurrently — 12–25 workers, resumable from a content ledger.

FRAME 02

Analyze

scan_media.py

Reads EXIF/GPS and FFprobe metadata, scores sharpness and exposure, and perceptual-hashes photos and video thumbnails alike for near-duplicates.

FRAME 03

Story

build_story.py

Groups by capture day, keeps the strongest stills, caps videos to one or two per day (a stable per-day coin flip, not a fixed rule), and drops any clip that already appeared once.

FRAME 04

Locate

fetch_album.py

Reverse-geocodes embedded GPS through OpenStreetMap Nominatim — rate-limited, cached, no API key — for the venue/city badge on each scene.

FRAME 05

Score

fetch_music.py

Finds a CC0 / YouTube-safe instrumental on Wikimedia Commons, verifies its license, then mutes every clip's own noise for one continuous cue underneath.

FRAME 06

Render

render.py

FFmpeg composites every card, still and clip through a content-addressed cache — an interrupted run resumes for free — into landscape and portrait finals.
