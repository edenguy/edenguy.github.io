### Where an integrated interactive app can differentiate (evidence-based)
- **Hebrew / RTL**: no agency eLearning or calculator above is verified as offering Hebrew; PADI has only announced it as "soon to follow" ([pros-blog.padi.com](https://pros-blog.padi.com/?p=90290)).
- **Theory + calculators in one place**: agencies deliver video/quiz eLearning; calculators are separate apps that show results, with only one (Dalton) exposing formulas ([apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/tr/app/id6759679904)). An app that teaches the derivation and lets the learner manipulate FO2/PO2/depth live fills a visible gap.
- **CNS/OTU tracking is rare in free tools**; Borrett and most calculators omit it ([borrett.id.au](https://www.borrett.id.au/interests/nitrox.php)).
- **Simulated analyzer and labeling practice**: agencies require analyzing real cylinders in person ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/?p=76362)); a simulator can rehearse the workflow (calibrate, flow, read, label) before the shop visit, without claiming to replace it.
- **Price transparency**: agency eLearning pricing is shop-dependent and opaque; a free or clearly priced learning tool is a differentiator.

## Part B — Scientific and operational references

### B1. Formulas and unit conventions
- **Metre sea water (msw)**: by definition 10 msw = 1 bar; fsw is based on standard gravity and sea-water density 64 lb/ft³; 1 atm ≈ 33 fsw ≈ 14.7 psi; msw/fsw are gauge pressures ([Wikipedia: Metre sea water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_sea_water)). EN 13319 gives 1 msw = 10.0381 kPa ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_sea_water)). With density 1027 kg/m³ and g = 9.80665, 101325 Pa corresponds to ≈10.06 m of seawater (arithmetic; density-dependent) ([Wikipedia: Ambient pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambient_pressure)). **App convention**: teach P_abs(bar) = depth_m/10 + 1 and P_abs(ata) = depth_ft/33 + 1, and label them as training conventions, not physical constants.
- **Dalton's law**: total pressure = sum of partial pressures; **PO2 = FO2 × P_abs** (BSAC workbook; RAID) ([people.bath.ac.uk](https://people.bath.ac.uk/bssjd/Files/BSAC%20Nitrox/Part2/SD_Nitrox_Workshop_Student_Workbook_V10.pdf), [diveraid.com](https://diveraid.com/john-dalton-a-man-of-many-parts/)).
- **MOD** (metric): MOD = 10 × (PO2_limit / FO2 − 1) msw; imperial: 33 × (PO2_limit / FO2 − 1) fsw; e.g. EAN36 at 1.4 → 28.9 msw ([Wikipedia: MOD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_operating_depth)).
- **EAD** (metric): EAD = (D + 10) × (FN2 / 0.79) − 10; imperial: (D + 33) × (FN2 / 0.79) − 33; e.g. EAN36 at 27 m → 20 m, at 90 ft → 67 ft ([Wikipedia: EAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equivalent_air_depth), [tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/tdi-diver-news/nitrogen-exposure-limits-and-equivalent-air-depth-ead/)).
- **Best mix**: FO2 = PO2_limit / P_abs(target depth) (rearrangement of Dalton; BSAC workbook; Borrett rounds the result down) ([people.bath.ac.uk](https://people.bath.ac.uk/bssjd/Files/BSAC%20Nitrox/Part2/SD_Nitrox_Workshop_Student_Workbook_V10.pdf), [borrett.id.au](https://www.borrett.id.au/interests/mod.php)).
- **Rounding**: BSAC tables round MOD down (EAN32 @1.4: 33.8 m calc → 33 m table) "introducing a further safety factor" ([people.bath.ac.uk](https://people.bath.ac.uk/bssjd/Files/BSAC%20Nitrox/Part2/SD_Nitrox_Workshop_Instructor_Manual_v11.pdf)); PADI instructors report tables give 110 ft for EAN32 and teach rounding down ([scubaboard.com](https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=4142942), forum — **not verified against PADI materials**); TDI EAD tables use "actual or next greater depth" ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/tdi-diver-news/nitrogen-exposure-limits-and-equivalent-air-depth-ead/)). **App rule: always floor MOD, ceil EAD and best-mix FO2 down.**

### B2. PO2 planning limits (training standards, not physics)
- **PADI**: 1.4 ata/bar maximum, 1.6 ata/bar contingency, in Enriched Air materials ([factually.co summary](https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/padi-dan-maximum-po2-limits-depths-d0d821); [scribd study guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/380348671/Nitrox) — secondary; **exact PADI wording not verified**). DAN notes "PADI has proposed a limit of 1.4 ATA for open-circuit nitrox" ([dan.org](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/oxygen-toxicity/)).
- **RAID**: "planned maximum PO2 of 1.4 bar and a contingency PO2 of 1.6 bar" ([diveraid.com](https://diveraid.com/?p=25068)).
- **DAN**: "the old limit of 1.6 ATA has largely been replaced with 1.4 ATA"; respect MODs at ≤1.4 ATA and keep exertion low ([dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/stick-to-your-maximum-operating-depth/)).
- **NOAA**: 1.6 ATA is the top of the normal-exposure table (45 min); exceptional-exposure limits "for use only in lifesaving operations" ([dan.org](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/oxygen-toxicity/), [scubaboard quoting the manual](https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=5632574) — **not verified against the manual text**).
- **Others**: Divesoft "Working/active phase 1.4 ATA; Controlled decompression 1.6 ATA" ([divesoft.com](https://www.divesoft.com/en/blog/po-limits-1-4-1-6-when-to-use-which-and-why)); WorkSafe NZ recommends working PPO2 max 140 kPa (1.4 bar) ([worksafe.govt.nz](https://registers.worksafe.govt.nz/dmsdocument/35980-bulletin-breathing-oxygen-and-enriched-mixtures-while-diving/latest)); BSAC uses 1.4 bar for deco and lower for Sports Divers/exertion ([people.bath.ac.uk](https://people.bath.ac.uk/bssjd/Files/BSAC%20Nitrox/Part2/SD_Nitrox_Workshop_Student_Workbook_V10.pdf)). **SSI's PO2 wording: not verified.**
- Caveat for content: risk rises with exercise and CO2 ([dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/stick-to-your-maximum-operating-depth/)); seizures reported after short exposures >1.3 ATA in exercising divers ([dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/understanding-oxygen-toxicity/)).

- **CNS % method**: CNS% = Σ (minutes at PO2 / single-exposure limit at that PO2) × 100; e.g. 45 min at 1.6 = 100%, i.e. 2.22 %/min at 1.6 and ≈0.67 %/min at 1.4 ([scubaboard.com](https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=3817434), [scubadoctor.com.au](https://www.scubadoctor.com.au/downloads/cns_percentage_tracking_v1-1.pdf)). Shearwater interpolates the NOAA table and decays CNS at the surface with a 90-min half-life (80% → 40% after 90 min; ≈0% after ~9 h) ([shearwater.com](https://shearwater.com/blogs/community/shearwater-and-the-cns-oxygen-clock)). A Scubapro document says the NOAA manual (2013 printing, p. 4-27) cites a 120-min half-time ([divetable.info](https://www.divetable.info/skripte/G2_TEK_OX_TOX.pdf)) — **conflict; do not hard-code surface credit for recreational planning; NOAA notes such credit "is seldom taken into account for planning recreational dives"** ([scubaboard quoting NOAA](https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=5330852)).
- Exceptional-exposure column (e.g. 1.6 → 120 min, 1.4 → 160 min) exists but is emergency-only; never show it as a planning limit ([dan.org](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/oxygen-toxicity/), [scubaboard.com](https://scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=6328050)).

### B4. OTU / UPTD
- Origin: UPTD defined at U. Pennsylvania (Bardin & Lambertsen 1970; Clark & Lambertsen 1970); 1 unit = 1 min at PO2 1.0 ata ([frontiersin.org](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2019.01297/epub)).
- Formula (REPEX form): **OTU = t × ((PO2 − 0.5)/0.5)^0.83**, PO2 in bar, threshold 0.5 bar — Hamilton, SPUMS J 1997 ([dhmjournal.com](https://www.dhmjournal.com/images/IndividArticles/27March/Hamilton_SPUMSJ.27.1.43-47.pdf)). The REPEX report: Hamilton, Kenyon, Peterson, Butler, Beers, NURP Technical Report 88-1A, May 1988 ([repository.library.noaa.gov](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/60673/noaa_60673_DS1.pdf)). Note some clinical sources write the UPTD slope as −1.2 (DMAC 35) — a different fit; use the 0.83 form and say so ([dmac-diving.org](https://www.dmac-diving.org/guidance/DMAC35.pdf)).
- Daily limits (REPEX): 850 OTU for a single day, 700/day for 2 days, flattening to 300/day for missions >10 days ([scubaboard summary](https://www.scubaboard.com/community/goto/post?id=5336564), [divetable.info](https://www.divetable.info/DWSD/DWSD_II.pdf)); R `scuba` package: 1500 OTU tolerable/day minus 650 reserved for recompression ⇒ 850 working ([rdocumentation.org](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/scuba/versions/1.11-1/topics/oxtox)). **Verify the multi-day table against the NURP PDF before publishing.** Shearwater argues UPTD should not drive planning ([shearwater.com](https://shearwater.com/blogs/community/uptd-calculations-not-used)); present OTU as informational for recreational EANx.

### B5. Oxygen toxicity signs
- **CNS (Paul Bert effect)**: DAN lists flashing lights/tunnel vision, tinnitus, confusion, lethargy, nausea/vertigo, numbness/tingling, muscular twitching esp. lips; "in many instances convulsions present with no precursors" ([pros-blog.padi.com (DAN staff)](https://pros-blog.padi.com/?p=92354), [dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/understanding-oxygen-toxicity/)). Mnemonic **ConVENTID / VENTID-C**: Convulsions, Vision, Ears, Nausea, Twitching, Irritability, Dizziness ([getoncourse.ai](https://getoncourse.ai/revision/indian-medical-pg/physiology/altitude-and-diving-physiology/oxygen-toxicity/), [jsomonline.com PJ Med handbook](https://www.jsomonline.com/Flipbook/PJMED/files/basic-html/page201.html)). Treat as a memory aid; emphasize that seizure may be the first sign.
- **Pulmonary (Lorrain Smith effect)**: described 1899; long/low-PO2 exposure; airway inflammation, dry cough, chest burning, shortness of breath; "like a bad case of the flu", rarely permanent ([dan.org](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/oxygen-toxicity/), [dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/understanding-oxygen-toxicity/)).

### B6. Oxygen-clean / oxygen-service thresholds
- **US "40% rule"**: equipment not exposed to >40% O2 above intermediate pressure "need not be O2 clean or service rated" ([divegearexpress.com](https://www.divegearexpress.com/library/articles/nitrox-ready)); DAN calls it "a long-held misconception" that "likely stems from misinterpretations of OSHA guidelines", and notes US DOT classifies >23.5% O2 as an oxidizer ([dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/oxygen-cleaning-of-dive-gear-a-two-part-series/)). A PHMSA interpretation treats 25–40% as higher fire risk than air ([phmsa.dot.gov](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/sites/phmsa.dot.gov/files/legacy/interpretations/Interpretations/2011/110238.pdf)).
- **CGA G-4.1 (2018, 7th ed.)**: cleaning required for surfaces contacting >23.5% O2; applicable with modification to cylinders, valves, regulators ([blog.ansi.org](https://blog.ansi.org/ansi/cga-g-4-1-2018-cleaning-equipment-oxygen/)).
- **Europe — EN 144-3:2003**: M26x2 outlet thread for cylinders "containing breathable nitrox gas with an oxygen content greater than 22 % or oxygen"; 200-bar and 300-bar variants; transition ended 2008 ([evs.ee](https://www.evs.ee/en/evs-en-144-3-2003), [online.standard.no](https://online.standard.no/en/ns-en-144-3-2003), [divernet.com](https://divernet.com/scuba-gear/regulators/nitrox-connections-which-way-will-the-m26-take-us/)). Adoption is uneven (UK HSE voted against; recommendation status) ([divernet.com](https://divernet.com/scuba-gear/regulators/nitrox-connections-which-way-will-the-m26-take-us/)).
- Partial-pressure blending always exposes cylinder/valve to 100% O2 → must be O2 clean regardless of final mix ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/2012/06/nitrox-tank-preparation-marking-and-identification/)). **Content rule: say "requirements differ by country and fill method; follow your fill station and local regulations."**

### B7. Oxygen analyzers
- Principle: galvanic (electrochemical) fuel cell producing current proportional to PO2 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-galvanic_oxygen_sensor), [biopac.com](https://www.biopac.com/?p=125659)).
- Calibration: "Air calibration is essential before every use" and must be at the same atmospheric pressure as measurement (Analox O2EII Pro manual) ([scubadoctor.com.au manual PDF](https://scubadoctor.com.au/downloads/other-manuals/analox-O2-EII-Pro-Technical%20Manual-MO2-865.pdf), [nuvair.com Analox manual](https://erp.nuvair.com/manuals/Analox-Mini-O2DII-Manual.pdf)); sensors are typically calibrated at 20.9% air and/or 100% O2 ([biopac.com](https://www.biopac.com/?p=125659)); humidity/altitude shift the air point ([uniquegroup.com spec](https://www.uniquegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/O2EII20Pro.pdf)).
- Accuracy: Analox O2EII Pro "± 1% of reading, ± 0.2% of O2", range 0.1–100% ([uniquegroup.com](https://www.uniquegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/O2EII20Pro.pdf)).
- Cell life: 4–5 years in air (Analox); 24–32 months (other industrial cells) ([uniquegroup.com](https://www.uniquegroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/O2EII20Pro.pdf), [instrumart.com](https://core.instrumart.com/assets/GPR-2600-Manual.pdf)).
- Flow sensitivity: flow must be 1–5 L/min (Nuvair), 0.5–2 L/min for some models; pressure on the cell raises the reading and can damage it; use a flow restrictor ([nuvair.com](https://nuvair.com/products/analyzers/oxygen), [nuvair.com](https://www.nuvair.com/pro-o2-remote-analyzer.html), [divesoft.com](https://www.divesoft.com/en/blog/a-divers-guide-to-analyzing-nitrox-the-right-way)).

### B8. Cylinder labeling conventions
- Minimum label content: O2 %, MOD, analyst name/initials, date of analysis ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/iti/how-to-label-your-nitrox-tank/)); one convention: MOD large in the middle, O2 % top-left, date top-right, fill pressure bottom-left, initials bottom-right ([diveraid.com](https://diveraid.com/?p=19724)).
- Green/yellow "Nitrox / Enriched Air" wrap identifies contents only; it "does NOT indicate the oxygen cleanliness" of cylinder or valve ([divegearexpress.com](https://www.divegearexpress.com/nitrox-tank-wrap-decal-large)). Use the measured FO2, not the nominal blend, for MOD ([gravitee.io](https://gravitee.io/corpus/gen-2018/scuba-gas-management/nitrox-blend-validation.html)).

### B9. Publicly stated course performance requirements (general terms only)
- PADI: analyze a cylinder, complete an enriched air log, set a dive computer for nitrox; two dives optional ([padi.com](https://padi.com/courses/enriched-air-diver?irra=14656)).
- SDI: analyze ≥2 cylinders and label per local practice; log ≥1 analysis with MOD and O2; program a nitrox computer 22–40%; ≥80% exam ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/?p=76362)).
- SSI: EAN40 requires practical sessions "Analyzing Cylinders" and "Calibration Review" plus final exam ([divessi.com](https://www.divessi.com/en/blog/enriched-air-nitrox-32-program-upgraded-1048)).
- TDI Nitrox: demonstrate EAD calculations and perform oxygen analysis ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/tdi/get-certified/nitrox-diver/)).
- RAID: plan to PO2 1.4 with 1.6 contingency, mixes 22–40% ([diveraid.com](https://diveraid.com/?p=25068)).
Safe app phrasing: "Agencies commonly expect certified EANx divers to analyze their own cylinder, label it, calculate or look up MOD, and set their computer to the measured mix."

## Part C — Claims the app must NOT make, and disclaimer wording

**Do not claim**
1. That completing the app certifies, qualifies, or "counts toward" any certification. Agency theory eLearning itself does not certify; an instructor/center completes training and issues the card ([padi.com](https://padi.com/courses/enriched-air-diver?irra=14656), [diveraid.com](https://diveraid.com/nitrox/)).
2. That the app substitutes for agency training, instructor review, or hands-on cylinder analysis ([tdisdi.com](https://www.tdisdi.com/?p=76362)). Existing calculators explicitly disclaim this ("not intended for actual dive planning... refer to your certifying agency") ([apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nitrox-helper/id1451300850); [apps.apple.com Diveplan](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6451319559)).
3. That the app is, or replaces, a dive computer or analyzer, or that its CNS/OTU figures are a safety guarantee; NOAA/agency limits are standards with margins, not thresholds of safety ([dan.org](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/stick-to-your-maximum-operating-depth/)).
4. That any PO2 limit, O2-clean threshold, or label format is "the law"; they differ by agency and country (Part B2, B6).
5. Agency affiliation, logos, or wording that implies endorsement by PADI, SSI, RAID, SDI/TDI, NAUI, DAN or NOAA.

**Recommended disclaimer (show at first launch and under every calculator)**
> Nitrox Academy is an independent educational tool for recreational divers. It is not a certification course, is not affiliated with or endorsed by any training agency, and does not replace instruction from a qualified Enriched Air instructor. Its calculators and oxygen-exposure estimates are for learning and practice only; they are not a dive computer and must not be used to plan or conduct real dives. Always analyze your own cylinder with a calibrated analyzer, label it, set your dive computer to the measured mix, and follow your training agency's limits and local regulations. Depth–pressure conversions use the common training conventions (10 m/33 ft per atmosphere); oxygen exposure limits shown are agency/NOAA training standards, not guarantees of safety.
