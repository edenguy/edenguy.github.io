Reel 2 · The Cut

From album link to finished film

Six steps happen automatically once you share the album — no timeline to touch, no software to learn.

STEP 01

Gather

Downloads every photo and video in the album, in the order you took them — and picks up right where it left off if a connection drops.

STEP 02

Curate

Checks focus and exposure on every shot and quietly drops the blurry, near-duplicate, and repeated ones.

STEP 03

Shape

Groups the trip by day, keeps your strongest photos, limits how many clips appear per day, and never shows the same clip twice.

STEP 04

Locate

Reads the location tagged on each photo and labels every scene with the city or place it was taken.

STEP 05

Score

Adds a royalty-free instrumental soundtrack that plays continuously underneath, so your clips' own background noise doesn't take over.

STEP 06

Render

Puts it all together into two finished films — one widescreen cut for the TV, one vertical cut for your phone.

Reel 3 · The Map

A spatial roadmap, not just a slideshow

Every GPS-tagged photo and clip in the album — not just the ones that made the cut — plots onto a real map in capture order. Each day's own travel crawls in as the film reaches it, building on every day already shown; the film closes on the complete route animating end to end, with the total kilometers, days, stops, and countries covered.

EVERY DAY

A day-title card becomes a small map instead of plain text: yesterday's route already drawn, today's crawling in fresh — framed tight to that day's own area so an early day doesn't vanish in a map sized for the last one.

THE FINALE

A closing recap animates the whole trip's route across a real map, opening on the headline numbers rather than saving them as an end payoff.

REAL GEOGRAPHY

Real OpenStreetMap tiles, darkened and kept vivid — not an abstract line on a blank canvas — labelled with the same place names already used for on-screen location badges.

FLIGHT DETECTION

A leg too long for a single day's drive draws as a dashed arc, the familiar flight-path convention, instead of a straight line.

Reel 4 · The Director's Chair

You stay in charge of the story

TaleMontage makes the first cut automatically, then hands you the chair. Review what it picked before a single frame renders, and steer the result with a few plain choices.

REVIEW FIRST

A contact sheet of the selected photos and clips, with running time, lets you drop or restore moments before the film is rendered. Your own album captions carry into the film.

SHAPE THE FILM

Pick a target length from three to twelve minutes, a calm, balanced or energetic pace, a reduced highlights reel or the full album in order, and short highlights or full videos.

PEOPLE VS. SCENERY

Tilt the selection toward fewer or more faces. Sharp scenery can still beat a mediocre portrait, and the reverse holds too.

YOUR SOUNDTRACK

Choose a music style: classical and acoustic, rock, metal, electronic, or any. Every track is royalty-free and carries its license in the film's credits. Don't like the pick? Swap the style and re-score.

EVERY SCREEN

Widescreen for the TV, vertical for the phone, or both from the same run. Preview renders come back fast before you commit to the final quality.

NO AI GUESSWORK

Selection, sequencing and rendering follow fixed, published rules. There are no model calls anywhere in the pipeline, so the same album gives the same film every time.
