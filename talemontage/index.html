The cut

From album link to finished film

Six steps happen automatically once you share the album — no timeline to touch, no software to learn.

01

Gather

Downloads every photo and video in the album, in the order you took them — and picks up right where it left off if a connection drops.

02

Curate

Checks focus and exposure on every shot and quietly drops the blurry, near-duplicate and repeated ones.

03

Shape

Groups the trip by day, keeps your strongest photos, limits how many clips appear per day, and never shows the same clip twice.

04

Locate

Reads the location tagged on each photo and labels every scene with the city or place it was taken.

05

Score

Adds a royalty-free instrumental soundtrack that plays continuously, so your clips’ own background noise doesn’t take over.

06

Render

Puts it all together into two finished films — one widescreen cut for the TV, one vertical cut for your phone.

The map

A spatial roadmap, not just a slideshow

Every GPS-tagged photo and clip in the album — not just the ones that made the cut — plots onto a real map in capture order. Each day’s own travel crawls in as the film reaches it; the film closes on the complete route, with the total kilometers, days, stops and countries covered.

The director’s chair

You stay in charge of the story

TaleMontage makes the first cut automatically, then hands you the chair. Paste the album link, confirm it’s the right one, and steer the result in a few short steps.

Confirm your album

Paste a share link and see the album’s real cover, name and item count before anything starts. A link can carry the album inside it, so you can send someone a ready-to-go page.

Review first

A thumbnail grid of every photo and clip, grouped by day, shows what was picked and the running time. Untick to drop a moment, or clear every shot with faces in one click. On a phone, press and hold, then drag to select a whole block. Every tick is saved as you go.

Length & pace

Pick a target length from three to twelve minutes and how long each photo stays. A slider re-fills the selection toward the running time, best shots first, while your hand-picked choices stay put.

People vs. scenery

Tilt the selection toward fewer or more faces. Sharp scenery can still beat a mediocre portrait, and the reverse holds too.

Your soundtrack

Choose a style: classical and acoustic, rock, metal, electronic, or any. Every track is royalty-free and credited. Don’t like the pick? Swap the style on the finished film and it re-scores.

Every screen

Widescreen for the TV, vertical for the phone, or both from the same run. Turn on a soft crossfade between photos, or keep straight cuts.

Picks up where you left

Refresh or reopen the link and you land back on your build’s progress, review or finished films. Closing a tab doesn’t cancel it on the spot.

No AI guesswork

Selection, sequencing and rendering follow fixed, published rules. No model calls anywhere in the pipeline, so the same album gives the same film every time.
